Commercial Refrigeration Repair – Common Problems

A temperature spike can be caused by airflow restrictions, mechanical component wear, or electrical control errors. Here are the 8 most frequent commercial refrigeration problems we solve across Miami.

Key refrigeration failure modes:

  • Walk-in cooler or freezer box warming up
  • Evaporator coil completely encased in solid frost & ice
  • Compressor short cycling or tripping high pressure safety
  • Cracked, torn door gaskets letting humid kitchen air in
  • Clogged condensate drains leaking water across floors

1. Refrigerator Not Cooling

Common Problems:
• The refrigerator fails to maintain the required cabinet temperature.
• Restricted condenser airflow or dirty coils can reduce cooling performance.
• Compressor problems or faulty thermostat sensors may affect temperature control.
Our Inspection:
Inspect condenser fan operation and clean accessible condenser coils.
Assess compressor performance and refrigerant operating pressures.
Check electronic temperature sensors and thermostat calibration.

2. Freezer Temperature Rise

Common Problems:
• Freezer temperatures rise above the required storage setting.
• Defrost heater or timer problems may allow excessive ice accumulation.
• Expansion valve or refrigerant flow issues can reduce cooling performance.
Our Inspection:
Test defrost heater continuity and termination controls.
Check defrost timer operation and sequence.
Inspect expansion valve operation and investigate possible refrigerant restrictions.

3. Repeated Safety Shutdowns

Common Problems:
• Commercial refrigeration compressors shut down unexpectedly.
• High-pressure safety cutouts or thermal overloads may interrupt operation.
• Restricted condenser airflow or excessive operating temperatures can contribute to shutdowns.
Our Inspection:
Inspect condenser coils and fan airflow.
Check electrical contactors and compressor operating conditions.
Test safety controls and thermal overload operation.

4. Nonstop Unit Operation

Common Problems:
• The refrigeration unit runs continuously without reaching its temperature setting.
• Worn door seals can allow warm air to enter the cabinet.
• Temperature sensor problems or reduced compressor performance may prevent normal cycling.
Our Inspection:
Inspect compressor operation and refrigeration performance.
Check door seals and identify air leakage around cabinet openings.
Test and recalibrate digital temperature probes where required.

5. Walk-In Holding Failures

Common Problems:
• Walk-in coolers or freezers struggle to maintain their required temperatures.
• Damaged insulation or poorly sealed panel joints can allow heat and moisture to enter.
• Refrigeration circuit or evaporator performance problems may reduce cooling capacity.
Our Inspection:
Inspect box insulation, panel joints, and door sealing.
Assess condensing unit operation and evaporator performance.
Check expansion valve operation and investigate suspected refrigerant leaks.

6. Door & Gasket Leaks

Common Problems:
• Cracked or hardened door gaskets allow warm, humid air into refrigerated compartments.
• Moisture entering the cabinet can contribute to condensation and ice buildup.
• Damaged seals may cause longer refrigeration cycles and temperature fluctuations.
Our Inspection:
Inspect magnetic door gaskets and identify damaged sealing areas.
Adjust sagging hinges and check door alignment.
Replace unsuitable or worn gaskets and assess the need for PVC strip curtains.

7. Evaporator Coil Icing

Common Problems:
• Ice accumulates on the evaporator coil and restricts airflow.
• Excessive ice buildup reduces the system's ability to remove heat.
• Defrost heater failures, faulty termination switches, or refrigerant problems may contribute to icing.
Our Inspection:
Inspect defrost heaters, termination switches, and fan delay controls.
Test defrost components under operating conditions.
Evaluate airflow and refrigerant operating conditions to identify the cause.

8. Condensate & Water Leaks

Common Problems:
• Water collects inside walk-in coolers or around refrigeration equipment.
• Blocked drain lines can cause condensate to overflow onto floors.
• Cracked drain pans or failed pan heaters may contribute to water accumulation.
Our Inspection:
Inspect and clear condensate drain lines.
Check drain pans and test pan heaters where installed.
Inspect heat-trace cables and replace damaged drainage components as needed.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Commercial Refrigeration FAQs

Direct answers to common questions regarding commercial refrigeration repair, walk-in coolers, and maintenance in Miami:

What commercial refrigeration equipment can be repaired?

We service walk-in coolers, walk-in freezers, reach-in refrigerators, commercial display cases, prep tables, undercounter units, bottle coolers, and remote condensing systems for commercial facilities.

Why is my walk-in cooler running but not getting cold?

Common causes include an iced-over evaporator coil, low refrigerant charge from a system leak, dirty or grease-coated condenser coils, a malfunctioning expansion valve (TXV), or worn compressor valves.

Why does my commercial freezer keep building up ice?

Excess ice usually points to a defrost system failure—such as a bad defrost timer, burned heating element, or failed termination thermostat—or air infiltration from torn door gaskets.

What causes a commercial refrigerator to run constantly?

Nonstop operation is often triggered by dirty condenser coils, worn door gaskets allowing continuous warm air infiltration, refrigerant undercharge, or a faulty temperature controller.

Can a refrigerant leak cause a commercial refrigerator to stop cooling?

Yes. Refrigerant is required to absorb and transfer heat. If a leak develops, cooling performance drops, the evaporator coil may ice over partially, and the compressor can overheat.

When should a commercial refrigeration compressor be replaced?

Replacement is required if the compressor has an internal mechanical lockup, grounded electrical windings, acid contamination, or severely damaged valves that cannot maintain pressure.

What is included in commercial refrigeration maintenance?

Maintenance includes chemical coil cleaning, fan motor checks, door gasket inspection, temperature control calibration, defrost cycle review, electrical testing, and condensate drainage inspection.

Should I repair or replace a commercial walk-in cooler?

The decision depends on the condition of the refrigeration machinery, box panel and door insulation integrity, repair history, and parts compatibility. A diagnostic assessment helps determine whether repairing the unit or planning a replacement is more cost-effective.

What information should I provide when requesting refrigeration repair?

Provide the equipment type, brand and model if available, current box temperature, normal temperature setpoint, symptoms (clicking, ice, warm air), business location, and photos if possible.

Do you install new commercial walk-in coolers and freezers?

Yes. We install new walk-in coolers and freezers, including box assembly, refrigeration equipment matching, remote condensing unit placement, electrical connections, and drainage installation.

Do commercial refrigeration installation projects require permits in Miami?

Yes. Commercial refrigeration installations, walk-in additions, and major mechanical alterations typically require mechanical permit applications, plan review, and inspections from the local building authority.
