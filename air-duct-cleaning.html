DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA

Air Duct Cleaning – Common Indicators & Inspection

Not every dusty vent means the entire duct system needs professional cleaning. The important question is whether there is significant contamination inside the ductwork or another HVAC condition causing the symptom.

Key indicators evaluated:

  • Debris or particulate discharge from registers
  • Persistent dust accumulation around grilles
  • Musty or stale odors when the blower engages
  • Post-renovation drywall dust in return plenums
  • Visual deposits inside trunk lines and boots

1. Dust or Debris Coming From AC Vents

Common Problems:
• Visible dust or debris blows out of supply registers when the AC operates.
• Loose particles inside ductwork may circulate into living spaces.
• Excessive internal dust deposits can accumulate in affected duct sections.
Our Inspection:
Distinguish ordinary room dust from substantial internal duct buildup.
Inspect affected duct sections for excessive dust and debris.
Include only confirmed affected duct runs in the cleaning plan.

2. Dust Keeps Collecting Around Supply & Return Vents

Common Problems:
• Dust repeatedly collects around supply and return vent covers.
• Cleaning the grilles does not prevent visible buildup from returning.
• Poorly fitted filters may allow dust to bypass filtration and enter the ductwork.
Our Inspection:
Inspect register openings and accessible return ductwork.
Evaluate filter rack fitment for possible air bypass.
Identify internal dust accumulation that may contribute to recurring buildup.

3. Musty or Stale Smell When the AC Turns On

Common Problems:
• Musty or stale odors appear when the air conditioner starts.
• Moisture around the evaporator coil or condensate drain pan may contribute to odors.
• Organic buildup inside the blower housing can also be a source.
Our Inspection:
Trace the odor to its actual source within the HVAC system.
Check the evaporator coil, drain pan, and blower housing for moisture or buildup.
Determine whether duct cleaning is appropriate or another cleaning service is needed.

4. Dust and Debris After Home Renovation

Common Problems:
• Drywall sanding, cutting, and flooring work release fine construction dust.
• Running the HVAC system during renovation can draw dust into return ductwork.
• Construction debris may settle inside accessible plenums and duct branches.
Our Inspection:
Inspect accessible duct openings, plenums, and branches.
Use visual or camera checks to assess internal construction dust.
Determine whether debris has entered the ductwork or remains around register grilles.

5. Visible Buildup Inside Air Ducts

Common Problems:
• Heavy dust or lint deposits are visible through accessible duct openings.
• Different supply and return sections may have different levels of buildup.
• A single dirty section does not necessarily mean every duct run needs cleaning.
Our Inspection:
Inspect accessible supply trunks and return duct runs independently.
Assess the location and extent of visible dust deposits.
Recommend cleaning only for sections that genuinely require attention.

6. Excessive Pet Dander & Airflow Resistance

Common Problems:
• Pet hair and dander can accumulate inside return plenums and ductwork.
• Organic debris may combine with moisture and form heavier deposits.
• Significant buildup inside duct sections may contribute to airflow restrictions.
Our Inspection:
Inspect affected duct sections for accumulated pet hair and dander.
Assess the extent of deposits before selecting a cleaning method.
Use suitable negative-pressure extraction for compatible duct materials.

DETAILED SCOPE

What's Included in an Air Duct Cleaning Service?

The exact scope should be agreed upon before cleaning begins. These are the main HVAC duct components that may be assessed and included where accessible and appropriate.

01

Supply Air Duct Cleaning

Supply ducts carry conditioned air from the equipment to rooms. Cleaning focuses on dislodging confirmed buildup from agreed supply branches and capturing loosened material under negative vacuum pressure.

02

Return Air Duct Cleaning

Return ducts carry air from living areas back to the air handler. Return-side cleaning goes deep beyond wiping the visible grille, reaching accessible runs and plenums where heavy lint gathers.

03

Air Vent, Register & Grille Cleaning

Supply registers and return grilles are carefully unfastened, cleaned on both sides with non-abrasive degreasers, dried, and securely reinstalled to restore a fresh, dust-free appearance.

04

Main Trunk Lines and Plenums

Main trunk lines distribute air across multiple branch runs. Plenums connect the duct network to equipment. Accumulated debris in accessible trunk lines is captured using heavy-duty vacuum collars.

05

Blower Compartment Assessment

The blower moves all air through the ducts. Dust on blower fins or motor housing is evaluated. If blower wheel detailing is required, its specific scope is clearly stated before work proceeds.

06

Evaporator Coil & Pan Inspection

Indoor coils cool and dehumidify Miami air. We visually inspect the coil face and condensate pan. If coil sanitization or drain line flushing is recommended, it is quoted as a transparent option.

CLARITY & SAFETY

Air Duct Cleaning vs. Air Duct Sanitizing

Duct cleaning and duct sanitizing are not interchangeable services. Customers should understand the difference before agreeing to chemical treatments or additional charges.

Physical Duct Cleaning

Physical duct cleaning targets dust, debris and accumulated material that can be dislodged from surfaces and collected using suitable equipment.

  • Removes solid particulates, drywall residue, and hair
  • Uses physical brushes and HEPA negative-air collectors
  • No chemical sprays required for routine dust removal

Antimicrobial Sanitizing

Sanitizing is an optional antimicrobial fogging considered only in specific microbial situations. It is never a compulsory substitute for physical removal.

  • Only EPA-registered HVAC botanical products utilized
  • Customer is informed of product, application area and limits
  • Never sold as an automatic upsell or whole-house cure
TRANSPARENT PRICING

What Affects Air Duct Cleaning Cost in Miami?

Air duct cleaning cost depends on the amount of work required, not simply the number of vent covers visible in a room.

Number of HVAC Systems

A property with two or three separate air handlers requires independent vacuum connections and cleaning setups for each network.

Vents & Register Count

Total supply and return register counts help establish labor hours and the overall duct branch footage to be cleared.

Ductwork Accessibility

Accessible attics with crawlboards require different labor compared to tight ceiling chases or multi-story condo utility closets.

Duct Material & Condition

Rigid sheet metal tolerates standard mechanical brushes, while flexible and internally insulated duct board require delicate soft-whip care.

Level of Contamination

Heavy renovation drywall dust or extensive pet hair accumulation requires multi-pass agitation compared to light seasonal dust.

Additional HVAC Components

Adding blower wheel detailing, chemical coil cleaning, or condensate line treatment can be bundled transparently with your duct service.
