When a pellet stove stops working, the problem is not always obvious. It may turn on but fail to ignite, feed pellets inconsistently, shut down during operation or produce less heat than usual. You may also notice unusual smoke, error messages or a blower that no longer runs correctly.

These symptoms can involve different parts of the stove. DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA

Pellet Stove Repair – Common Problems

A pellet stove relies on several systems working together: fuel delivery, ignition, combustion airflow, temperature sensing and room-air circulation. A fault in one area can affect the entire startup or heating cycle.

Core operating systems checked:

  • Electric igniter element & burn pot seating
  • Auger feed motor, gearbox & fuel delivery
  • Combustion exhaust & room convection blowers
  • Vacuum pressure switch, hose & vent draft
  • Control board logic & safety limit switches

1. Why Won't Your Pellet Stove Ignite?

Common Problems:
No Flame During Startup: The stove begins its startup sequence, but pellets do not ignite.
Pellets Without Ignition: Pellets reach the burn pot, but no flame develops.
Failed Ignition Cycle: The stove stops or displays a fault before establishing a fire.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Test the igniter's electrical supply and resistance.
Inspect the burn pot for ash buildup and restricted combustion airflow.
Verify pellet delivery and check the startup sequence for control faults.

2. Why Is Your Pellet Stove Not Feeding Pellets?

Common Problems:
Empty Burn Pot: The hopper contains pellets, but none reach the combustion chamber.
Interrupted Pellet Supply: Pellets feed inconsistently, causing the flame to weaken or go out.
Feed System Failure: An obstruction, safety interlock, or electrical fault may interrupt pellet delivery.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Inspect the hopper outlet, feed chute, and auger path for blockages.
Check feed-system safety switches and electrical connections.
Test the auger motor and control signals to identify why feeding has stopped.

3. Why Is Your Pellet Stove Auger Not Turning?

Common Problems:
Auger Jam: Compressed or obstructed pellets prevent the auger from rotating.
Motor Failure: The auger motor receives a command but cannot drive the feed mechanism.
Drive Mechanism Damage: A worn gearbox or damaged coupling interrupts pellet movement.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Inspect the auger shaft and feed mechanism for mechanical obstructions.
Test motor operation, gearbox condition, and electrical connections.
Check safety interlocks before determining whether a component needs repair or replacement.

4. Why Does Your Pellet Stove Keep Shutting Down?

Common Problems:
Shutdown After Startup: The stove starts but stops before reaching normal operation.
Flame Goes Out: The fire cannot remain established during the heating cycle.
Unexpected Safety Shutdown: A vacuum, temperature, or airflow-related fault interrupts operation.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Review the stove's error codes and shutdown sequence.
Inspect vacuum hoses, pressure switches, door gaskets, and combustion airflow.
Evaluate exhaust draft and combustion blower performance before replacing safety components.

5. Why Is Your Pellet Stove Producing Smoke or Poor Draft?

Common Problems:
Smoke Entering the Room: Smoke escapes into the living area instead of moving through the exhaust system.
Weak or Unstable Flame: Restricted exhaust airflow can interfere with proper combustion.
Unusual Exhaust Odors: Venting problems or leaking connections may affect safe operation.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Inspect exhaust pipes, vent connections, and termination points for restrictions or leaks.
Evaluate combustion blower operation and draft pressure.
Check door seals and other combustion chamber connections for air leakage.
Safety Note: If smoke enters your home, stop operating the stove and arrange a professional inspection before using it again.

6. Why Is Your Pellet Stove Producing Weak Heat?

Common Problems:
Low Heat Output: The stove burns pellets, but the room does not warm as expected.
Poor Heat Distribution: The stove produces heat, but warm air does not circulate effectively.
Reduced Heat Transfer: Ash accumulation inside the heat exchanger can interfere with heat delivery.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Inspect heat exchanger passages for ash buildup and airflow restrictions.
Test convection blower operation and room-air circulation.
Evaluate pellet feed performance and combustion conditions.

7. Why Is Your Pellet Stove Blower Not Working?

Common Problems:
No Warm Air Circulation: The convection blower fails to distribute heated air into the room.
Poor Combustion Airflow: A faulty exhaust blower can interfere with the stove's draft and combustion.
Unusual Motor Noise: Worn bearings or motor problems may cause buzzing, vibration, or grinding.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Identify whether the issue involves the combustion blower or convection blower.
Test motor operation, wiring connections, and electrical supply.
Inspect relevant control board outputs and motor components.

8. Why Is Your Pellet Stove Displaying an Error Code?

Common Problems:
Vacuum or Draft Warning: The stove cannot confirm the airflow conditions required for operation.
Proof-of-Fire Fault: The control system does not detect the expected temperature or flame condition.
High-Limit Shutdown: The stove stops because a temperature safety limit has been triggered.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Identify the error code using the manufacturer's service information for the specific model.
Test relevant sensors, safety switches, and control system feedback.
Trace the underlying electrical or mechanical fault instead of replacing components based on the code alone.

SYSTEM COMPONENTS

Pellet Stove Components That May Need Repair

The repair depends on which part has failed and what caused it to stop working. These are the primary mechanical, combustion, and electrical components inspected.

1

Electric Igniter

The igniter heats the area where pellets are introduced so that combustion can begin. If it fails or lacks power, the stove cannot produce a flame.

2

Auger & Feed Motor

The auger moves pellets from the hopper into the burn pot. A jam, worn motor, damaged gearbox or electrical fault interrupts fuel delivery.

3

Combustion & Exhaust Blower

Moves exhaust gases through venting and supports combustion airflow. A weak blower causes poor draft, smoke problems, and safety shutdowns.

4

Convection Room Blower

Circulates heated air into the living space. If faulty, the stove burns fuel without delivering warmth effectively into the room.

5

Vacuum Switch & Hose

Monitors airflow pressure. A damaged hose, restricted vent, weak blower, poor seal or faulty switch interrupts safety signals.

6

Temperature Sensors

Sensors monitor operating temperatures. A faulty sensor, loose wire or tripped high-limit switch disrupts heating cycles.

7

Control Board

Coordinates the stove operating sequence, motors, igniter and safety inputs. We verify wiring and sensors before replacing boards.

8

Door & Ash-Pan Gaskets

Gaskets maintain critical airtight seals. If worn or degraded, the stove struggles to maintain draft and triggers pressure errors.

MAINTENANCE & AIRFLOW

Pellet Stove Cleaning & Airflow Inspection

Cleaning is an important part of pellet stove care, but it should not be used as a catch-all explanation for every fault. Ash buildup can restrict airflow and heat transfer; it cannot repair a failed motor, damaged wiring, faulty sensor or defective control board.

01

Burn Pot and Firebox Cleaning

Ash and hardened residue collect in the burn pot, air openings and firebox. Blocked openings starve the flame of combustion air. We check for accumulated ash, obstructed air holes, and firebox metal deterioration.

02

Heat Exchanger & Internal Passages

Fly ash collects around heat exchanger tubes and internal baffles. As buildup increases, it reduces heat transfer into your living room. Thorough cleaning restores proper thermodynamic efficiency.

03

Exhaust Vent and Draft Inspection

The exhaust vent carries combustion gases outside. Ash accumulation or loose joints affect draft and create smoke issues. Cleaning stove interior without checking the exhaust leaves root draft causes untouched.

04

Gasket and Airtight Seal Inspection

Door, ash-pan and glass gaskets prevent unmetered air from entering the combustion chamber. We inspect for frayed rope material, loose latches, and loss of seal compression to restore vacuum integrity.

DECISION GUIDANCE

Should You Repair or Replace Your Pellet Stove?

A failed component does not automatically mean the whole stove needs replacing. The decision should consider structural condition, parts availability, repair history and the cost of proposed work.

When Repair Makes Sense

Repair is practical when the problem is isolated and the main structure remains in serviceable condition:

  • A failed igniter that can be replaced with compatible OEM spec
  • An auger or feed motor problem with available replacement parts
  • A combustion or convection blower that can be serviced or swapped
  • A damaged gasket, pressure hose, or accessible wiring connection
  • A control-board fault where a suitable replacement is available

When Replacement May Be Better

Replacement deserves consideration when core structural integrity is compromised:

  • The firebox steel or cast iron main structure has cracked or deteriorated
  • Essential electrical boards or motors are obsolete and no longer available
  • Multiple expensive component failures are occurring together
  • Total repair estimate approaches the installed cost of a new stove
  • Recurring failures require frequent, costly emergency service visits
TRANSPARENT ESTIMATES

What Affects Pellet Stove Repair Cost?

Pellet stove repair cost depends on the fault, model and work required. An igniter replacement is different from diagnosing an intermittent control board or repairing an auger assembly.

Failed Component

The exact part, OEM availability, and whether it can be reconditioned or needs complete replacement directly affect costs.

Stove Make & Model

Different brands use proprietary computerized control systems, specialized sensors, and varying mechanical drive layouts.

Diagnostic Time

Intermittent shutdowns or sensor glitches require methodical electrical testing compared to a straightforward failed igniter.

Component Accessibility

Some internal repairs require extensive disassembly of combustion side panels, firebrick, and exhaust shrouds to access motors.

Venting & Airflow Work

Draft restrictions, damaged flue connections, or flue pipe soot blockages may require corrective labor beyond the stove itself.

Compound System Faults

An auger failure may be secondary to a vacuum leak or worn door gasket. A thorough diagnostic resolves all contributing issues.
