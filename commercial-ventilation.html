Commercial Exhaust & Ventilation Repair – Common Problems

A commercial ventilation problem can stem from electrical failures, belt drive wear, clogged ductwork, or pressure imbalances. Here are the 8 most frequent ventilation issues we resolve for Miami businesses.

Key ventilation failure modes:

  • Exhaust fan not starting or tripping circuit breaker
  • Kitchen hood failing to capture cooking smoke & steam
  • Severe negative building pressure pulling doors shut
  • Loud bearing rumble, fan vibration & squealing belts
  • Make-up air unit blower failure & motorized damper faults

1. Exhaust Fan Not Starting

Common Problems:
• The commercial exhaust fan fails to turn on.
• Kitchen or warehouse ventilation stops when the fan becomes inoperative.
• Electrical supply faults, failed contactors, or motor winding problems may prevent startup.
Our Inspection:
Trace electrical feeds and check incoming line voltage.
Test starter contactors, control relays, and thermal overloads.
Inspect local disconnect switches and motor windings.

2. Weak Exhaust Fan Airflow

Common Problems:
• The exhaust fan runs but does not remove enough air.
• Heat, fumes, or stale air remain inside the commercial space.
• Loose drive belts, clogged dampers, or incorrect impeller rotation may reduce airflow.
Our Inspection:
Inspect belt tension and fan wheel condition.
Check dampers and remove accumulated debris where accessible.
Measure airflow and evaluate system static pressure.

3. Hood Not Capturing Smoke

Common Problems:
• Cooking smoke escapes around the edges of the commercial kitchen hood.
• Grease buildup on baffle filters can restrict exhaust airflow.
• Poor airflow balance or cross-drafts can interfere with smoke capture.
Our Inspection:
Check hood airflow and capture performance.
Inspect grease baffle filters for excessive resistance.
Evaluate nearby supply-air diffusers and cross-drafts affecting the hood.

4. Excessive Commercial Heat

Common Problems:
• Commercial kitchens remain excessively hot during equipment operation.
• Exhaust airflow may be insufficient for the heat generated by cooking equipment.
• Hot or poorly controlled make-up air can add to indoor heat loads.
Our Inspection:
Evaluate appliance heat loads against exhaust requirements.
Check exhaust airflow and make-up air delivery.
Assess make-up air temperature and distribution where applicable.

5. Negative Building Pressure

Common Problems:
• Entrance doors become difficult to open or close due to pressure imbalance.
• Exhaust fans remove more air than the building replaces.
• Excessive negative pressure can draw humid outdoor air through building openings.
Our Inspection:
Measure and compare exhaust and make-up air volumes.
Inspect make-up air equipment and outside-air delivery.
Adjust airflow balance to help maintain appropriate building pressure.

6. Fan Noise & Roof Vibration

Common Problems:
• Commercial exhaust fans produce excessive noise or roof vibration.
• Worn bearings or shaft movement can create mechanical noise.
• Unbalanced fan wheels and loose mounting components may increase vibration.
Our Inspection:
Inspect pillow-block bearings, shaft play, and fan wheel balance.
Replace worn mechanical components where required.
Evaluate mounting hardware and vibration isolators.

7. Make-Up Air Unit Outage

Common Problems:
• The make-up air unit stops supplying replacement air.
• Building pressure may drop when exhaust fans continue operating.
• Failed supply motors, control relays, or broken drive belts can interrupt operation.
Our Inspection:
Troubleshoot supply blower motors and contactors.
Inspect intake louvers, bird screens, and air-proving switches.
Check interlock controls and replace damaged drive belts where necessary.

8. Damper & Actuator Faults

Common Problems:
• Motorized dampers fail to open, close, or adjust properly.
• Faulty actuators or airflow sensors disrupt ventilation control.
• Damper problems can prevent proper airflow adjustment as building occupancy changes.
Our Inspection:
Test 24V actuators, linkage rods, and control signals.
Inspect damper blades for binding or mechanical damage.
Check BMS control operation and inspect fire damper components where applicable.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Commercial Ventilation FAQs

Direct answers to common questions regarding commercial ventilation, exhaust fans, and kitchen airflow in Miami:

What is included in commercial ventilation service?

Commercial ventilation service includes diagnostic evaluation of exhaust fans, motors, belts, electrical controls, ductwork static pressure, make-up air equipment, and overall building airflow balancing.

Do you repair commercial exhaust fans in Miami?

Yes. We diagnose and repair all types of commercial exhaust fans including rooftop upblast fans, inline blowers, sidewall exhausters, and commercial kitchen hood ventilation systems across Miami-Dade and Broward.

Why is my commercial kitchen exhaust fan running but not removing smoke?

Common causes include slipped or worn drive belts, a damaged or grease-clogged fan impeller wheel, closed or obstructed dampers, grease filter blockages, or reverse fan motor rotation.

What does a make-up air unit do?

A make-up air unit replaces air that has been pulled out of a building by commercial exhaust systems. It helps maintain indoor air balance, controls pressure differentials, and supports overall HVAC efficiency.

Can poor make-up air cause negative pressure?

Yes. If exhaust systems remove more air than the building takes in, negative pressure develops. This can cause doors to be difficult to open, backdrafts, indoor humidity problems, and uncomfortable hot spots.

Why is my restaurant kitchen hot even when the AC is running?

Commercial kitchens produce substantial radiant heat. If exhaust capture is weak, make-up air is supplying hot untempered air directly into the space, or airflow balance is improper, standard cooling cannot keep pace.

Do you install commercial kitchen exhaust hoods?

We provide commercial ventilation and exhaust services for kitchen setups, including duct connections, exhaust fan installation, make-up air matching, and airflow balancing according to mechanical plans.

How often should commercial ventilation equipment be inspected?

High-volume commercial kitchens benefit from quarterly or bi-monthly inspections of belts, bearings, motors, and grease containment. Standard commercial buildings should be serviced at least twice annually.

Do commercial ventilation projects require permits in Miami?

New commercial ventilation installations, hood additions, structural curb penetrations, and major duct redesigns generally require mechanical permits and engineering review from the local building department.

What information should I provide when requesting commercial ventilation service?

Provide the type of facility (restaurant, warehouse, retail), reported symptoms (smoke, noise, negative pressure), equipment accessibility (roof ladder, hatch), and manufacturer nameplate data if available.
