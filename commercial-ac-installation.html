UPGRADE INDICATORS

Signs It Is Time for Commercial AC Replacement

Commercial cooling systems work under constant demand in Miami. When equipment begins to fail repeatedly or can no longer satisfy building cooling loads, replacement provides a dependable solution.

Key replacement factors evaluated:

  • Equipment cannot maintain temperature during peak heat
  • Frequent breakdowns disrupting business operations
  • Tenant buildouts, expanded floorplans, or altered use
  • Spike in commercial monthly utility costs
  • Severe coastal rooftop corrosion and obsolete refrigerant

Commercial Site Evaluation

We assess your building layout, internal heat loads, roof curb condition, and duct connections to engineer the ideal system.

1. System Unable to Meet Demand

When outdoor temperatures reach the 90s, an aging or undersized commercial unit runs continuously without reaching the thermostat setpoint, leaving spaces uncomfortably warm.

Our Solution: We perform manual cooling load calculations accounting for occupancy, electronics, and solar gain to specify correctly sized high-efficiency equipment.

2. Frequent Costly Breakdowns

When commercial equipment requires repairs several times a year, cumulative maintenance costs and emergency service disruptions exceed the value of the unit.

Our Solution: Installing a modern high-reliability commercial system backed by comprehensive manufacturer warranties eliminates operational uncertainty.

3. Building Renovations & Additions

Subdividing large open office areas, adding commercial kitchen capacity, or adding square footage alters thermal loads, making original equipment obsolete.

Our Solution: We engineer multi-zone commercial packaged or split configurations tailored to the new floor plan and occupancy schedules.

4. Escalating Utility Power Bills

Commercial equipment older than 12-15 years operates at low SEER/IEER ratings. Deteriorated compressors and fouled coils draw excessive power to maintain minimal cooling.

Our Solution: Modern high-efficiency commercial equipment with variable-speed ECM blower motors and staged compressors substantially lowers operating overhead.

5. Phased-Out R-22 Refrigerant

Older commercial systems operating on banned R-22 refrigerant face prohibitive recharge costs whenever a leak occurs, alongside difficult part sourcing for obsolete compressors.

Our Solution: Replacing aging units with environmentally compliant R-410A or next-generation refrigerant systems ensures long-term serviceability.

6. Coastal Rust & Roof Cabinet Wear

In South Florida's salt-air marine environment, rooftop cabinets, base pans, and condenser fins corrode over time, risking structural failure and roof leaks.

Our Solution: We install commercial rooftop units featuring epoxy-coated corrosion-resistant coils, heavy-gauge galvanized cabinets, and custom curb adapters.

Existing Equipment & Layout Changes

  • Assessing existing electrical panels, disconnects, and breaker capacity

CODE COMPLIANCE

Commercial HVAC Installation Requirements in Miami

Commercial HVAC installations in South Florida must adhere to Florida Building Code, Miami-Dade High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) standards, and municipal requirements:

Mechanical Permits & Plan Review

Commercial mechanical replacements require permit filings and subsequent inspections with local building departments.

  • Filing mechanical permit applications with Miami-Dade County or municipal building officials
  • Submitting equipment technical cut sheets and Florida Product Approval numbers
  • Scheduling final mechanical, electrical, and structural inspections

Hurricane Wind-Load Anchoring

Rooftop equipment in South Florida must withstand hurricane-force winds up to 175+ mph in accordance with Miami-Dade HVHZ requirements.

  • Engineered tie-down straps and heavy-gauge curb anchoring
  • Structural roof curb fastening to concrete or steel roof decking
  • Vibration isolators to minimize sound transmission into occupied suites
TRANSPARENT PRICING

What Affects Commercial AC Installation Cost in Miami?

Commercial installation pricing depends on equipment tonnage, crane logistics, and existing duct infrastructure:

1. Equipment Type & Tonnage Capacity

Commercial units range from 3 to 25+ tons. High-efficiency variable-capacity RTUs and units with integrated economizers involve higher equipment costs but deliver substantial power savings.

2. Rooftop Crane Hoisting & Rigging

Lifting multi-ton equipment onto commercial roofs requires crane permitting, street closure coordination where necessary, and licensed rigging crews to ensure safe equipment placement.

3. Curb Adapters & Duct Transitions

Fabricating custom insulated curb adapters to match new RTU openings to old roof curbs eliminates the need to reroof or modify primary structural steel openings.

4. Removal, Disposal & Permitting

Environmentally compliant refrigerant recovery, safe removal and recycling of the old unit, and municipal permit and inspection fees are detailed in our written estimates.
