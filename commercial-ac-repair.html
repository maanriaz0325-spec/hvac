DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA

Commercial AC Repair – Common Problems

A commercial cooling problem can appear in different ways. Sometimes the entire system stops. In other cases, the equipment continues running but cannot maintain temperature in one part of the building.

Common commercial failure modes evaluated:

  • Compressor, condenser fan & motor faults
  • Tripped circuit breakers & electrical safety cutoffs
  • Condensate drain overflows & ceiling leaks
  • Short-cycling and rapid on/off thermostat cycling
  • Frozen coils & electronic leak detection

Fast Business Diagnostics

We assess the unit, electrical supply, and refrigerant circuits before recommending a repair so you never pay for guesswork.

1. Running but Not Cooling

Common Problems:
• The commercial AC runs, but the building remains warm.
• Cooling capacity drops due to possible refrigerant leaks, compressor faults, or dirty coils.
• A failing blower motor may prevent conditioned air from reaching occupied areas.
Our Inspection:
Assess refrigerant operating pressures and subcooling.
Inspect evaporator and condenser coils, along with fan operation.
Check for refrigerant leaks before recommending any refrigerant recharge.

2. AC Has Stopped Working

Common Problems:
• The commercial AC unit will not start or respond to its controls.
• The system shuts down unexpectedly during business hours.
• Electrical supply faults, failed contactors, or tripped safety controls may interrupt operation.
Our Inspection:
Test safety interlocks, contactors, and capacitors.
Check incoming supply voltage and electrical connections.
Diagnose repeated breaker trips before attempting to reset the system.

3. Uneven Cooling or Weak Airflow

Common Problems:
• Meeting rooms, offices, or retail sections remain warmer than other areas.
• Some vents deliver weak airflow while others receive adequate cooling.
• Restricted filters, closed zone dampers, or disconnected ductwork may affect air distribution.
Our Inspection:
Check blower assembly operation and filter restrictions.
Inspect zone dampers and accessible duct connections.
Determine whether the problem originates at the air handler or within the duct distribution system.

4. Water Leaking From System

Common Problems:
• Water collects around commercial HVAC equipment or mechanical rooms.
• Condensate leaks appear through ceiling tiles or near indoor units.
• Clogged drain lines, cracked drain pans, or failed condensate pumps may cause water overflow.
Our Inspection:
Trace the leak to its source and inspect the condensate drainage system.
Clear clogged drain lines and traps where required.
Test pump floats and inspect damaged drain pans for repair or replacement.

5. Keeps Turning On and Off

Common Problems:
• The commercial AC repeatedly starts and stops instead of completing normal cooling cycles.
• Frequent cycling creates inconsistent indoor temperatures.
• Faulty thermostat sensors, safety cutoffs, or restricted return airflow may trigger shutdowns.
Our Inspection:
Review the system's operating sequence and cycling pattern.
Evaluate pressure sensors, safety controls, and thermostat calibration.
Check airflow conditions to identify the cause of abnormal cycling.

6. Unusual Noise or Vibration

Common Problems:
• Grinding, squealing, buzzing, or rattling comes from commercial AC equipment.
• Worn motor bearings or slipping drive belts may produce mechanical noise.
• Loose fan blades, brackets, or compressor components may cause vibration.
Our Inspection:
Isolate the source of mechanical noise and vibration.
Inspect belts, bearings, fan assemblies, and mounting brackets.
Adjust, secure, or replace affected components as needed.

7. Frozen Commercial AC Coil

Common Problems:
• Ice forms across the evaporator coil and restricts airflow.
• Cooling performance drops as the coil becomes covered in ice.
• Restricted filters, blower problems, or low refrigerant levels may contribute to freezing.
Our Inspection:
Safely thaw the frozen coil before further testing.
Inspect filter banks, blower operation, and airflow conditions.
Check refrigerant operating conditions to identify the underlying cause.

8. Refrigerant Leak in Circuit

Common Problems:
• Refrigerant loss reduces the system's ability to cool commercial spaces.
• The AC may struggle to maintain indoor temperatures.
• Repeated refrigerant top-ups without leak repairs can lead to recurring cooling problems.
Our Inspection:
Use suitable electronic leak detectors to locate suspected refrigerant leaks.
Inspect accessible refrigerant lines, coils, and copper connections.
Explain suitable repair or coil replacement options based on the confirmed leak location.

STRATEGIC DECISION

When Is Commercial AC Repair Better Than Replacement?

A breakdown does not automatically mean a multi-ton commercial system must be replaced. We evaluate equipment condition objectively:

When Commercial Repair Makes Sense

Repair is practical when the fault is identifiable, replacement parts are accessible, and the rest of the unit remains mechanically sound.

  • Electrical faults: contactors, capacitors, relays, or thermostats
  • Mechanical parts: fan motors, belts, bearings, or condensate pumps
  • Equipment is under 10-12 years old with good maintenance history

When Replacement Should Be Considered

Replacement is worth discussing when repair costs approach 50% of new unit value or repeated breakdowns disrupt business operations.

  • Catastrophic compressor burnout on aging commercial equipment
  • Extensive coil corrosion and obsolete R-22 refrigerant circuits
  • Building remodeling requires altered tonnage or zoning configurations
TRANSPARENT PRICING

What Affects Commercial AC Repair Cost in Miami?

There is no single flat repair fee for commercial systems. The estimate depends on the equipment, fault, and necessary labor:

1. Equipment Type & Tonnage

Split systems, multi-ton packaged units, and rooftop units have different component configurations, voltage requirements (208V/230V/460V 3-phase), and service access procedures.

2. Nature of the Fault

Replacing an electrical contactor or clearing a condensate drain trap involves modest labor compared to rebuilding a blower assembly or performing acid flushes on a burned-out compressor.

3. Parts Availability & Refrigerant

Common electrical components are stocked on our service trucks. Proprietary OEM circuit boards or large quantities of commercial refrigerant affect the overall repair quote.

4. Access & Site Procedures

Rooftop units requiring security escorts, specialized safety harnesses, or after-hours service windows are factored into the diagnostic and labor plan.
