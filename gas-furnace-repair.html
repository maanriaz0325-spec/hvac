DIAGNOSTIC & REPAIR SCOPE

Gas Furnace Repair Services in Miami

A gas furnace relies on several components working in the correct sequence. The thermostat calls for heat, the ignition system starts the burner, the flame is established and detected, and the blower circulates heated air through the home. A fault at any stage can interrupt heating.

Our gas furnace repair service focuses on diagnosing problems with existing gas-fired equipment, including:

1

Ignition failures & faulty furnace ignitors

Cracked silicon carbide or silicon nitride hot-surface ignitors and intermittent spark modules.

2

Flame sensor & burner operation problems

Oxidized flame sensor rods, burner tube corrosion, and improper microamp flame rectification signals.

3

Gas valve & related control issues

Faulty solenoid coils, gas pressure regulator malfunctions, and 24V control signal dropouts.

4

Furnace pressure switch & inducer motor

Draft inducer motor failures, blocked pressure ports, cracked sensing tubing, and open draft switches.

5

Blower motor & airflow-related faults

Direct-drive PSC or ECM blower motors, run capacitors, high-limit temperature sensor trips.

6

Electrical connections, control boards & safeties

Integrated furnace control (IFC) boards, flame rollout switches, auxiliary limit cutoffs, and wiring.

7

Heat exchanger & exhaust venting concerns

Combustion chamber inspection, flue pipe drafting analysis, and carbon monoxide prevention checks.

The repair required depends on the furnace model, its condition, and the fault identified. We focus on the actual cause rather than assuming that every furnace shutdown needs the same replacement part.

DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA

Gas Furnace Repair – Common Problems

A gas furnace can show several warning signs before it stops heating altogether. The important part is understanding where its operating sequence is being interrupted and whether the problem involves ignition, gas delivery, flame detection, airflow, or a safety control.

Key furnace stages inspected:

  • Thermostat demand & 24V gas controls
  • Draft inducer motor & draft pressure switch
  • Hot-surface ignitor & flame sensor rectification
  • Heat exchanger integrity & flue exhaust venting

Critical Safety Notice

If you smell natural gas, hear loud ignition bangs, or your carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave immediately and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.

1. Why Is Your Gas Furnace Not Turning On or Igniting?

Common Problems:
No Startup Response: The furnace does not respond when you raise the thermostat setting.
Inducer Runs Without Ignition: The draft inducer starts, but the burners never light.
Repeated Ignition Failure: The system attempts to start but stops before producing heat.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Verify the thermostat heating signal and electrical supply.
Test inducer operation and pressure-switch response.
Inspect ignitor resistance, gas valve operation, and control board safety signals.

2. Why Does Your Gas Furnace Shut Off Shortly After Ignition?

Common Problems:
Flame Starts Then Disappears: The burners ignite but shut down within a few seconds.
Repeated Startup Attempts: The furnace keeps attempting ignition without completing a heating cycle.
No Sustained Heating: The system cannot maintain burner operation long enough to heat your home.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Measure flame sensor microamp signal to evaluate flame detection.
Inspect the flame sensor for contamination and check burner grounding.
Evaluate gas supply and burner flame stability when the sensor signal is not the cause.

3. Why Are Your Gas Furnace Burners Not Lighting or Staying Lit?

Common Problems:
Ignitor Works but No Flame: The ignition system operates, but gas does not ignite at the burners.
Uneven Burner Flame: Some burners fail to light or produce an unstable flame.
Burner Shutdown: The flame starts but does not remain consistent during operation.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Inspect burner assemblies and crossover ports for blockage or deterioration.
Check gas valve operation and manifold pressure.
Evaluate combustion air supply and ignition sequence.

4. Why Is Your Gas Furnace Blowing Cold Air?

Common Problems:
Air Without Heat: The blower operates, but the burners are not producing sufficient heat.
Blower Runs After Burner Shutdown: Air continues circulating after a failed ignition or safety shutdown.
Interrupted Heating: The furnace repeatedly delivers cool air instead of maintaining the desired temperature.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Check burner operation and confirm whether the heating cycle is completing.
Test high-limit switches, thermostat fan settings, and blower timing.
Review control board fault codes to identify why heat production is interrupted.

5. Why Does Your Gas Furnace Keep Overheating or Short-Cycling?

Common Problems:
Frequent Shutdowns: The furnace stops before reaching the thermostat setting.
Restricted Airflow: Dirty filters or blocked return air can cause excessive internal temperatures.
Repeated Safety Trips: The high-limit switch interrupts burner operation to protect the equipment.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Measure duct static pressure and inspect filters and return-air pathways.
Test high-limit switch operation and temperature response.
Inspect blower wheel condition and motor performance to identify airflow-related overheating.

6. Is Your Furnace Pressure Switch or Inducer Motor Causing Ignition Failure?

Common Problems:
Pressure Switch Does Not Close: The furnace cannot confirm the required draft before ignition.
Inducer Motor Problems: Weak or failed inducer operation prevents proper exhaust movement.
Repeated Pressure Faults: The furnace displays a pressure-switch error even when the switch itself may not be defective.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Measure draft pressure using appropriate diagnostic equipment.
Inspect the inducer assembly, exhaust vent, and pressure-switch tubing.
Check condensate drainage where applicable and test control board signals.

7. Why Does Your Gas Furnace Make a Loud Bang When Starting?

Common Problems:
Delayed Ignition: Gas may accumulate before the burners ignite, producing a sudden bang.
Dirty Burner Ports: Restricted crossover channels can prevent smooth flame transfer between burners.
Unusual Startup Noise: A loud ignition event may indicate a combustion or burner-related fault.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Inspect burner alignment and crossover ports.
Check ignition timing and gas manifold pressure.
Evaluate burner operation before returning the furnace to service.
Safety Note: Stop operating a furnace that produces repeated loud ignition bangs and arrange a professional inspection.

8. Could Your Gas Furnace Have a Heat Exchanger or Venting Problem?

Common Problems:
Visible Heat Exchanger Damage: Cracks, corrosion, or deterioration may compromise the combustion chamber.
Venting Restrictions: Blocked or damaged exhaust pathways can interfere with safe combustion.
Carbon Monoxide Concerns: Improper combustion or venting can allow dangerous gases to accumulate indoors.
How Our Technicians Diagnose It:
Inspect the heat exchanger using appropriate visual and borescope methods.
Evaluate exhaust draft and venting condition.
Perform combustion analysis and carbon monoxide testing where appropriate.
SYSTEMATIC DIAGNOSIS

What We Check During a Gas Furnace Repair

A gas furnace repair should begin with understanding how the equipment is operating and where its sequence is being interrupted. The inspection is directed by symptoms, furnace design, and findings rather than a one-size-fits-all parts checklist.

1

Thermostat & Power

We assess whether the furnace receives the call for heat, checking the 24V control transformer, door interlock switch, circuit breakers, and IFC board power.

2

Ignition Components

The ignitor and associated controls are checked when the furnace fails to light, including hot surface ignitor resistance (ohms) and spark ignition modules.

3

Burner & Flame Sensing

We assess burner operation, carryover alignment, and whether the flame-sensing system is correctly confirming flame rectification signals back to the main control board.

4

Gas Valve & Controls

Where indicated, gas-control operation, manifold pressure, and relevant electrical signals are assessed by certified technicians to verify proper fuel delivery without leaks.

5

Inducer & Pressure Switch

These components are verified when the furnace cannot establish draft before ignition, testing inducer amperage, fan wheel integrity, and pressure switch continuity.

6

Blower & Airflow

If the furnace runs without warming the home or repeatedly shuts down, blower capacitor strength, motor windings, fan wheel cleanliness, and filter restrictions are checked.

7

Heat Exchanger & Exhaust Venting

Where symptoms or inspection indicate a concern, relevant combustion and venting components are assessed for condition, cracks, corrosion, and safe flue gas discharge away from the property.

CRITICAL SAFETY PROTOCOLS

Gas Furnace Safety: When to Stop Using the System

A gas furnace combines fuel, electrical controls, ignition, and combustion. Some warning signs require immediate safety action rather than a routine repair appointment.

If You Smell Gas

Leave the building immediately and take everyone with you. Do not operate lights, switches, appliances, or phones inside. From a safe location outside, call 911 or your gas utility. Do not re-enter until emergency responders or the utility confirm it is safe.

If CO Alarm Sounds

Leave the property immediately and get fresh air. Call 911 from outside and do not return until the property has been assessed and declared safe. Carbon monoxide cannot be identified by smell; functioning detectors are essential.

Loud Ignition Bangs

Stop using the furnace and arrange a qualified inspection immediately. Repeated ignition noises can indicate delayed gas combustion that risks cracking the heat exchanger or causing fire hazards.

Overheat / Vent Damage

If you notice burning plastic smells, charred casing, or separated flue vent pipes, shut down electrical power to the furnace at the switch or breaker and arrange a certified inspection.

EXPERT GUIDANCE

Should You Repair or Replace Your Gas Furnace?

Not every furnace problem means the entire system needs to be replaced. A faulty ignitor, flame sensor, electrical connection, or individual component may be repairable when the rest of the furnace is in suitable condition.

Replacement deserves consideration when the equipment has extensive deterioration, recurring failures, unavailable parts, or a serious safety-related defect. We evaluate the entire system based on:

1. Age and Overall Condition

Gas furnaces typically last 15–20 years. Older equipment approaching this milestone with multiple worn components is often more economical to replace.

2. Heat Exchanger & Venting Safety

If the heat exchanger is cracked, rusted through, or venting is compromised, replacement is virtually always recommended due to carbon monoxide hazards.

3. Number & Frequency of Repairs

If you have needed multiple service visits over the past two heating seasons, the system's reliability is failing and replacement may be the smarter investment.

4. Parts Availability & Compatibility

Discontinued furnace models may require obsolete or hard-to-source parts, resulting in extended downtime and elevated repair expenses.

5. Repair Cost vs. Replacement Cost

If a repair costs close to 50% of a modern high-efficiency furnace, replacement offers superior warranty protection and significant monthly energy savings.

6. Likelihood of Further Significant Faults

We assess whether fixing one part (e.g., an inducer motor) will leave a failing blower motor or corroded burner assembly waiting to break down next.

TRANSPARENT PRICING

How Much Does Gas Furnace Repair Cost in Miami?

Gas furnace repair costs depend on the fault, the furnace model, the components involved, and the amount of work required. An ignitor replacement, a control-board problem, and a burner or venting issue are different jobs and should not be treated as if they have one standard price.

Type of Furnace Fault

The cause of the problem affects the diagnostic work and repair requirements. A furnace that fails during ignition may need a different inspection and repair from one that overheats or shuts down after running.

Replacement Parts and Compatibility

If a component has failed, the correct replacement must be compatible with the furnace. OEM electronic ignitors, specialized pressure switches, and model-specific control boards influence the estimate.

Access and Repair Work

The location of the furnace (attic closet, crawlspace, or dedicated mechanical room) and accessibility of affected components influence labor time and safety procedures.

Repair or Replacement Decision

If the furnace has multiple problems or a serious heat exchanger concern, consider whether a high-efficiency furnace replacement in Miami is more economical than repeated repairs. For property additions or new construction, we also engineer new furnace installations.
