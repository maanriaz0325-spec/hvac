DIAGNOSTIC SIGNS

Commercial Rooftop Unit (RTU) Repair – Common Problems

A rooftop unit combines cooling, fans, electrical components, controls, and condensate drainage in one packaged system. Describing what the equipment is doing helps narrow down the inspection.

Key rooftop failure modes:

  • Compressor runs but building remains warm
  • RTU not starting or unexpected safety shutoffs
  • Roof leaks, condensate pan overflow & ceiling stains
  • Excessive vibration, worn blower belts & bearings
  • Economizer & outside-air damper actuator faults

1. RTU Running but Not Cooling

Common Problems:
• The rooftop unit runs, but the building does not reach the desired temperature.
• Compressor problems or dirty condenser coils may reduce cooling capacity.
• Refrigerant pressure or thermostat control issues may affect system performance.
Our Inspection:
Assess refrigerant superheat and operating pressures.
Check compressor amp draw and condenser coil condition.
Inspect coil airflow and thermostat control signals to identify the cause.

2. Not Starting or Safety Shutdowns

Common Problems:
• The rooftop unit fails to start when cooling is requested.
• The RTU shuts down unexpectedly during operation.
• Electrical supply faults, blown control fuses, failed contactors, or safety trips may interrupt operation.
Our Inspection:
Follow the unit's operating sequence to identify where operation stops.
Test safety controls, high-limit switches, and electrical connections.
Check three-phase power supply for voltage loss or imbalance where applicable.

3. Commercial RTU Short Cycling

Common Problems:
• The rooftop unit starts and stops more frequently than normal.
• Frequent cycling causes inconsistent indoor temperatures.
• Faulty thermostat sensors, freeze protection controls, or restricted filters may trigger shutdowns.
Our Inspection:
Check thermostat sensor calibration and control operation.
Test low-pressure and freeze protection controls.
Inspect filters and airflow conditions to identify the cause of repeated shutdowns.

4. Weak Airflow & Uneven Cooling

Common Problems:
• Some areas receive insufficient conditioned air from the rooftop unit.
• Slipping blower belts or clogged filter banks can reduce airflow.
• Closed dampers and disconnected duct collars may interrupt air distribution.
Our Inspection:
Measure total external static pressure.
Inspect supply and return plenums for airflow restrictions.
Check blower belts, filter banks, and damper operation.

5. RTU Water Leaks & Condensate

Common Problems:
• Water leaks appear around rooftop HVAC equipment or inside the building.
• Clogged condensate traps and rusted drain pans may cause overflow.
• Poorly sealed roof curb transitions can allow water to enter ceiling spaces.
Our Inspection:
Inspect and clear condensate drain lines and P-traps.
Test overflow float switches and inspect drain pan condition.
Check roof curb flashing and transitions for potential water entry points.

6. Excessive Noise or Vibration

Common Problems:
• Rattling, grinding, or squealing noises come from rooftop equipment.
• Worn drive belts, loose pulleys, or failing motor bearings may cause mechanical noise.
• Loose access panels and unbalanced blower wheels can contribute to vibration.
Our Inspection:
Check drive belt tension, pulleys, and motor bearings.
Inspect blower wheels and unit access panels.
Evaluate vibration isolators and secure or replace affected components.

7. Frozen Evaporator Coils

Common Problems:
• Ice accumulates across the RTU evaporator coil.
• Frozen coils restrict airflow and reduce cooling performance.
• Dirty filters, blower problems, or refrigerant issues may contribute to coil freezing.
Our Inspection:
Safely thaw the frozen evaporator coil.
Inspect and replace clogged commercial filters where needed.
Check blower motor operation and refrigerant operating conditions.

8. Economizer & Outside-Air Dampers

Common Problems:
• Rooftop unit economizers fail to regulate outside air properly.
• Stuck damper blades or failed actuators can disrupt ventilation.
• Faulty sensors may allow excessive hot, humid outdoor air into the building.
Our Inspection:
Test enthalpy sensors and damper actuator operation.
Inspect damper blades, linkages, and control signals.
Verify minimum outside-air settings according to the unit's design and applicable ventilation requirements.

  • Economizers & Dampers: Outside-air modulating damper calibration and actuator checks
  • Condensate Drainage: P-trap clearing, drain pan slope inspection, and overflow shutoff floats

CLIMATE FACTORS

Commercial RTU Service for Miami Properties

Rooftop equipment in South Florida operates in an intense environment with extreme heat, continuous moisture, and coastal salt-air exposure:

Heat, Humidity & Condensate Drainage

Long cooling runtimes extract dozens of gallons of water per day. A clogged drain line or cracked pan quickly becomes an emergency ceiling issue.

  • Condensate line flushing and dedicated cleanout ports
  • Inspecting drain pan corrosion and auxiliary safety float switches
  • Checking roof membrane flashing around the unit curb

Salt-Air Exposure & Coastal Corrosion

Properties near the coast experience accelerated galvanic corrosion on coil fins, fasteners, and cabinet panels.

  • Regular chemical coil washes to remove corrosive airborne salt deposits
  • Epoxy coil coatings and stainless steel fastener upgrades
  • Hurricane tie-down bracket inspections per Miami-Dade HVHZ standards
TRANSPARENT PRICING

What Factors Affect Commercial RTU Service Cost in Miami?

Pricing depends on equipment capacity, component requirements, and roof accessibility:

1. Service Type: Repair vs. Maintenance vs. Replacement

Routine preventive maintenance involves predictable seasonal rates. Major component repairs or crane-assisted RTU replacements involve different labor and equipment scopes.

2. Failed Component & Parts Sourcing

Standard electrical contactors, capacitors, and blower belts are readily stocked. Proprietary commercial compressors or economizer boards affect parts pricing.

3. Roof Accessibility & Safety Requirements

Easy interior roof hatch access allows efficient service. High-rise rooftops or buildings requiring exterior scaffolding and security escorts require extra site logistics.

4. Single Unit vs. Multi-Unit Property

Properties servicing multiple rooftop units during a single scheduled visit benefit from bundled multi-unit commercial maintenance pricing.
